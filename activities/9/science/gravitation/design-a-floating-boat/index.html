How the model works · assumptions & teaching notes

The hull is an open rectangular box with uniform thickness t, vertical sides, and watertight seams. Outside volume V = LWD. Material volume = LWD − (L − 2t)(W − 2t)(D − t). Dimensions are in cm; divide cm³ by 1,000 for litres. Material densities are illustrative fixed values.

Hull mass = material volume × material density. In floating equilibrium, displaced-liquid mass equals total mass, so required draft = total mass × 1,000 ÷ (liquid density × L × W). Buoyant force equals total weight. Freeboard = D − draft. At the rim, maximum cargo = liquid density × outside volume − hull mass. For a 20% freeboard target, replace outside volume by 0.8 times that volume. Displayed capacities are clipped to zero if even the empty hull cannot meet the target.

The cargo–draft graph includes a mathematical extension above the rim to show why that loading fails. Once flooded, the boat is marked overloaded; the simulation does not calculate its subsequent depth or any floating pieces. No waves, leakage, tipping, cargo stability, deformation, or real-world safety certification is modelled. Cargo blocks are schematic. This is a design investigation, not a construction or boating safety guide.

Physics reference: OpenStax · Archimedes’ principle.
