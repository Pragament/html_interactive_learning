How the model works · equations, assumptions & teaching notes

This is a solid sphere rolling without slipping, with enough grip to maintain rolling. Static friction enables rolling; it is different from the energy-dissipating rolling resistance selected here. Surface names are illustrative, not measured material data. Air resistance is omitted. The ramp joins the flat track smoothly, without impact losses. A fixed ball radius is used; changing mass changes density.

With g = 9.8 m/s², θ = asin(height / ramp length), and the chosen rolling resistance coefficient C, the model uses resistance force Cmg cos θ on the ramp and Cmg on the flat. For a solid sphere, I = ⅖mr², so ramp acceleration a = (5/7)g(sin θ − C cos θ). If this is not positive, the ball stays at rest. Horizontal acceleration is −(5/7)Cg until it stops. Each segment uses s = ut + ½at² and v = u + at.

Energy accounting: potential = mgz; forward kinetic = ½mv²; rotational kinetic = ⅕mv²; dissipated energy is the remaining initial energy. A negative acceleration means decreasing speed here, not backward motion. With zero rolling resistance, the ball continues at constant speed on the flat. Runs end at rest or at the 8 m observation boundary; reaching the boundary does not mean stopping.
