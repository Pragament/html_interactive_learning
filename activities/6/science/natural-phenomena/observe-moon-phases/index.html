📖 Concept Guide (Key Scientific Learnings)
🔦 1. The Moon Produces No Light

Unlike the Sun and stars, the Moon is a non-luminous body. It does not produce its own light. The glowing Moon we see in the night sky is solely due to sunlight bouncing off its rocky surface like a giant mirror.

🌗 2. Always Half-Lit in Space

At any given moment, exactly 50% of the Moon is illuminated by the Sun. The side facing the Sun is always in bright daylight, while the opposite side is in dark shadow. This never changes!

🔄 3. Orbital Position Causes Phases

As the Moon revolves around Earth, our viewing angle changes. From Earth, we usually see only a portion of that illuminated half. When we see all of it, it is a Full Moon; when we see none, it is a New Moon.

🏐 4. The Half-Painted Ball Analogy

When holding a half-painted ball facing the Sun: The white painted half represents the sunlit hemisphere. As your friend moves in a circle around you, you see different portions of the white paint, creating identical phases!
