For teachers — classroom notes

Running it in class (about 10 minutes)

  • Project Step 1 first. Ask students to predict, before each tap, whether the bulb will glow.
  • This screen stands in for the real bench setup (battery, bulb, wires, and a gap to hold the test item) — use it as a warm-up or as a substitute when apparatus is not available.
  • Move to Step 2 only after all four materials have been tested at least once.
  • Step 3 is a good exit-ticket check before ending the lesson.

Discussion prompts

  • What is common between iron and zinc that is different from sulphur and coal?
  • Can you name another metal you see at home that would probably conduct electricity?
  • Why do electricians use plastic-covered wires instead of bare metal wires?

Notes

  • This activity treats coal as a poor conductor, matching the standard Class 6 bench result for a coal lump — students may later learn that graphite (a form of carbon) does conduct, which is a good extension question for stronger classes rather than a contradiction.
  • The activity runs entirely offline in this one file, with no internet or account needed.
