Water Prism

Explore how a mirror placed in water can help sunlight form a colourful spectrum on a wall.

Learning goal

Sunlight looks white, but it contains many colours. In this activity, water and a mirror help direct the sunlight so the colours can be seen on a wall.

Your task: Set up the model, choose a mirror angle, and observe the result.
☀ Safety

Never look directly at the Sun or into a bright reflected beam. In a real experiment, observe only the safe light spot on a wall and follow your teacher's instructions.

Teacher Guide

Suggested flow

  1. Ask: “What colours do you think are present in sunlight?”
  2. Show the setup and ask students to predict the result.
  3. Demonstrate the mirror in the water and direct sunlight safely.
  4. Let students observe the coloured spectrum on the wall.
  5. Ask students to name the colours they notice.
  6. Use the quiz as a quick check.

Simple explanation

Sunlight looks white but contains many colours. The water and mirror help arrange the light so the different colours can be seen on the wall as a spectrum.

Questions for discussion

  • What did you observe on the wall?
  • Why was the mirror placed in water?
  • Where did the colours appear?
  • What happens if there is no sunlight?

Safety reminder

Never direct students to look at the Sun or into a reflected beam. The observation should be made on a safe surface such as a wall.
