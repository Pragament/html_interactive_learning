How the model works · assumptions & teaching notes

Each square represents 1 m², and a side of one square represents 1 m. Area = width × length; perimeter = 2(width + length). Vegetable, flower, and herb areas form the displayed unsimplified ratio. Plant-space estimates use 4, 6, and 9 plants/m² respectively. These are illustrative spacing choices, not species-specific recommendations.

The daily model uses the selected climate demand in mm/day, multiplied by 1.0 for vegetables, 0.8 for flowers, and 0.7 for herbs. One mm over one m² equals one litre. Effective rain is 80% of rainfall; the rest is assumed not to enter storage. Net irrigation = max(0, plant demand − effective rain on planted area). Gross target = net irrigation ÷ delivery efficiency. This target balances daily use; initial soil storage can buffer temporary deficits.

Stored water begins at half the selected root-zone capacity. Each day, available water = previous storage + useful irrigation per planted m² + effective rain. Plant demand uses available water first; surplus fills storage up to capacity, then drains. A deficit slows model growth. Seed progress lasts 3 model days with enough water; subsequent leaf growth also depends on sunlight, reaching its maximum rate at 6 h/day. The model assumes suitable air, temperature, nutrients, and uniform conditions. Germination and growth timings, storage capacities, efficiency values, rainfall fraction, and crop factors are teaching assumptions.

Soil storage is not the whole story: this simple model omits infiltration speed, waterlogging, root differences, crop-specific biology, and changing weather. Clayey soil has greater model storage, but is not automatically best for every plant. Photosynthesis uses light, carbon dioxide, and water to produce sugars; this model only represents water and light limitations on later growth, not chemical rates. No yield prediction is made.

Illustrative costs: ₹40 per metre of boundary fencing, ₹35 per planted m² for bed preparation, and ₹2 per plant space for seeds. These are fixed classroom inputs, not market prices. Each plan export includes the complete layout, growing conditions, and daily water history. Saved-plan comparisons are temporary until exported.

Water-budget reference: FAO · Crop water needs. Model values are simplified teaching inputs rather than irrigation advice.
