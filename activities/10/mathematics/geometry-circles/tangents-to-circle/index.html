Tangents to a Circle

Explore, Construct and Verify

Interactive Construction

Move the external point P farther from or closer to the circle and observe the two tangents.

What to observe: Each tangent touches the circle at exactly one point. These points are called the points of contact.

Measurements

Radius ⟂ Tangent at point of contact
Key observation:
The two tangent segments drawn from the same external point have equal lengths.
Theorem:
The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.

Predict Before You Reveal

1. From an external point, how many tangents can normally be drawn to a circle?
2. If PA and PB are tangents from P, what is true?
3. What is the angle between the radius and tangent at the point of contact?

Explore Further

  • Move P closer to the circle. What happens to PA and PB?
  • Move P farther away. Do the tangent lengths remain equal?
  • Observe the angle between OA and PA.
  • Compare the two triangles formed by the radii and tangents.

Teacher Mode

Use the simulation as a guided discovery activity rather than giving the theorem first.

Suggested Classroom Flow

  1. Ask students to predict how many tangents can be drawn from an external point.
  2. Move P and ask students to compare PA and PB.
  3. Ask students to estimate the angle between OA and PA.
  4. Let students observe that the angle remains 90°.
  5. Ask students to formulate the tangent-radius theorem in their own words.

Discussion Questions

  • Why does the tangent touch the circle at only one point?
  • Why are PA and PB equal?
  • What happens when P lies on the circle?
  • What happens when P moves inside the circle?
  • How could you verify the theorem using a ruler and protractor?

Learning Outcome

Students should be able to identify a tangent, locate its point of contact, explain why the radius is perpendicular to the tangent, and state that tangents from the same external point are equal.
