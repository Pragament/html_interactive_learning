Find the HCF with Euclid's Division Algorithm

Euclid's Division Lemma says: for positive integers a and b, there exist unique whole numbers q and r such that a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r < b. Repeat the lemma on (b, r) until the remainder becomes 0 — the last non-zero remainder is the HCF.

Stepa = b × q + r
Try in class: Give students two numbers each. Ask them to predict how many steps the algorithm will take before working it out — does a bigger gap between a and b mean more steps or fewer?

Prime Factorisation Ladder

By the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, every composite number can be written as a product of primes in exactly one way (apart from the order of the factors). Build the ladder for two numbers by picking a prime that divides the number at each step — pick any valid prime, the final factorisation always comes out the same.

Try in class: Once both ladders are done, ask "which primes appear in both lists?" before revealing the HCF row — that's the whole idea of HCF by prime factorisation in one question.

Will It Terminate?

A fraction p⁄q in lowest terms has a terminating decimal expansion exactly when the prime factorisation of q contains only 2s and 5s. If any other prime divides q, the decimal is non-terminating and repeating. Predict first, then check.

Try in class: Show only the denominator (hide the numerator) and ask students to decide terminating vs. repeating from the denominator's factors alone — the numerator never matters for this question, once the fraction is in lowest terms.
