Trigonometric Ratios of Special Angles

Explore 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90° through triangles and exact values

1. Choose a Special Angle

For an angle θ in a right-angled triangle:

sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse
cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent

2. Interactive Triangle

θ = 45°

The triangle changes as you select a different special angle.

3. Exact Values

sin θ √2 / 2 ≈ 0.7071
cos θ √2 / 2 ≈ 0.7071
tan θ 1 ≈ 1.0000
Observe:

At 45°, the opposite and adjacent sides are equal, so sin 45° = cos 45° and tan 45° = 1.

4. How is the Value Obtained?

For 45°, consider an isosceles right triangle with equal legs of length 1.

Hypotenuse = √(1² + 1²) = √2
sin 45° = 1 / √2 = √2 / 2

5. Prediction Challenge

Before looking at the answer, predict the value of sin 45°.

6. Think & Explore

  • What happens to sin θ when θ increases from 30° to 60°?
  • Why are sin 30° and cos 60° equal?
  • Why is tan 45° exactly equal to 1?
  • Can you identify a pair of complementary angles with equal sine/cosine relationships?
  • Why is tan 90° not defined?

Teacher Mode

This activity can be used as a guided classroom exploration before students memorize the standard trigonometric table.

Learning Objectives

  • Recall the definitions of sin, cos and tan.
  • Understand the origin of special-angle values.
  • Connect side ratios with exact values.
  • Recognize relationships between complementary angles.

Suggested Classroom Flow

  1. Ask students to predict sin 30°.
  2. Select 30° and inspect the triangle.
  3. Repeat for 45° and 60°.
  4. Ask students to compare sin and cos values.
  5. Discuss why tan 90° is undefined.

Discussion Questions

  • Why does sin 30° equal cos 60°?
  • What changes when the angle increases?
  • Which sides become equal at 45°?
  • What happens to tan θ as θ approaches 90°?

Assessment Idea

Ask students to close their notebooks and predict three special-angle values. Then use the interactive model to verify their answers and explain the geometric reason.

Teacher Extension Activity

Put students into pairs. One student selects an angle while the other predicts all three ratios. The first student then changes the angle and checks the prediction.

Extension: Ask students to explain the relationship:

sin(90° − θ) = cos θ
