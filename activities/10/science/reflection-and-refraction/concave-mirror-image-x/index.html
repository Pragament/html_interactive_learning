❩ What is a Concave Mirror?

A concave mirror (converging mirror) is a spherical mirror whose reflecting surface is curved inwards, facing towards the centre of the sphere. It is capable of forming both real-inverted and virtual-erect images depending on the object's position relative to its focus.

Pole (P)

The geometric centre of the reflecting spherical surface. All distances are measured from here.

Principal Focus (F)

The point on the principal axis where rays parallel to the axis converge after reflection. PF = f.

Centre of Curvature (C)

The centre of the hollow sphere of which the mirror is a part. PC = R = 2f.

⚡ The 4 Standard Rules for Ray Tracing

Any two of these four standard rays can be used to determine the exact position and size of an image:

Rule 1 — Parallel Ray

A ray of light parallel to the principal axis passes through the Principal Focus (F) after reflection from a concave mirror.

Rule 2 — Focal Ray

A ray of light passing through the Principal Focus (F) emerges parallel to the principal axis after reflection.

Rule 3 — Centre of Curvature Ray

A ray passing through the Centre of Curvature (C) strikes the mirror normally (∠i = 0°) and reflects back along the exact same path.

Rule 4 — Pole Ray

A ray incident obliquely to the Pole (P) reflects obliquely such that angle of incidence = angle of reflection (∠i = ∠r).

📐 New Cartesian Sign Convention & Practical Applications

Cartesian Rules:

  • Pole (P) is taken as origin (0, 0).
  • Object is always placed on the left: u is always negative.
  • Focal length of concave mirror is always negative (f < 0).
  • Real images form on the left: v < 0.
  • Virtual images form behind the mirror on the right: v > 0.
  • Erect height is positive (h > 0); inverted height is negative (h < 0).

Everyday Uses:

  • Shaving / Makeup Mirrors: Face placed between F and P produces an erect, magnified virtual image.
  • Dentist Mirrors: Small concave mirror held close to teeth provides an enlarged upright view.
  • Torches & Headlights: Bulb placed at Focus (F) creates a powerful parallel beam of light.
  • Solar Furnaces: Large concave reflectors concentrate parallel sunlight at Focus (F) producing extreme heat.
