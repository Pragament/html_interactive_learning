What's going on

  1. First law: the incident ray, the refracted ray, and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.
  2. Second law (Snell's law): for a given pair of media, the ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction stays constant.
n = sin i / sin r
n is the refractive index of glass with respect to air.

Because the two faces of a rectangular slab are parallel, the ray bends once entering (toward the normal, since glass is denser) and bends back by the same amount leaving it. The angle of emergence equals the angle of incidence — the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray, just shifted sideways.

d = t · sin(i − r) / cos r
d is the lateral shift, t is the slab thickness.

Why two pins and not one? A single point can't tell you a direction. Two pins, placed apart, fix the exact line the ray travels along — the same reason surveyors and carpenters sight along two points rather than one.

Note for classroom use

Works on a shared screen for a whole-class walkthrough, or on individual devices for pairs to try a few angles each. A good sequence: try i ≈ 20°, 40°, and 60° with the same material, then compare how the lateral shift changes — and repeat once at a fixed angle while swapping between water, perspex, and flint glass to see how the shift depends on the medium.
