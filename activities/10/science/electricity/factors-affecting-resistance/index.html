For teachers — discussion prompts and notes

Running it in class (about 15 minutes)

  • Project the Explore tab. Change only the length first and ask the class to call out what happens to the ammeter before you release the slider.
  • Send students to Predict individually or in pairs. The reasoning after each answer matters more than the score.
  • Use Challenge as the closing task. Challenge 2 is the "why is a heater made of nichrome" moment.

Discussion prompts

  • Two wires are made of the same metal and are the same length, but one is twice as thick. Which has more resistance, and by what factor? (Four times less — area depends on the square of the diameter. This one catches most students.)
  • Why is the connecting wire in the lab made of thick copper, but the coil inside a heater made of thin nichrome?
  • An electric bulb draws less current once it has been on for a while. Why? (Filament temperature.)
  • If you stretch a wire to double its length, its area also halves. What happens to R? (It becomes four times larger — a good stretch question for stronger students.)

Common misconceptions to watch for

  • "A thicker wire has more metal, so it must resist more." Students confuse quantity of material with the width of the path.
  • Treating resistance as a property of the circuit rather than of the wire itself.
  • Assuming current is "used up" along the length of the wire.
  • Believing all materials behave the same with temperature — mention that semiconductors behave the opposite way, without going further at this level.

Notes on the model

  • The circuit uses a 6 V cell and a fixed 2.0 Ω lamp in series with the test wire, so that currents stay in a realistic range. Point this out if students ask why current does not go on rising forever as the wire is thickened.
  • Standard resistivities at 20 °C are used, with the temperature coefficient α applied as ρ(T) = ρ₂₀[1 + α(T − 20)]. The linear form is an approximation and is fine for this level.
